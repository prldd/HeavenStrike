# Resonance War - Narrative Foundation

> Working design document for AI-assisted development. This document
> defines the narrative intent, world-building rules, character
> philosophy, and campaign structure. It is guidance, not final script.

# Vision

Resonance War is an original tactical RPG about infrastructure, political
legitimacy, and the costs of rebuilding after collapse.

The story is driven by political consequences rather than chosen-one
mythology. The player solves practical problems while others determine
what those solutions mean politically.

## Core Themes

-   Repair versus control.
-   Practical action versus institutional precedent.
-   History is written by those who organize victory, not always those
    who achieve it.
-   Technology is never neutral.
-   Every victory creates new political consequences.
-   There are no purely good factions.

# The World

Centuries ago the world was unified by the **Caelian Empire**, ruled
from its capital **Caelis**.

Its civilization depended on **The Source**, a vast infrastructure
network that powered cities, transport, communication, and autonomous
machines.

Modern society believes the Empire collapsed.

The hidden truth is that it deliberately fragmented itself after a
crisis surrounding the Source. Records were destroyed, institutions
divided, and history rewritten.

Every modern faction inherited only part of the original system.

# Modern Factions

Coal, Steam, Solar, Wind, and Fusion are complete civilizations with
their own governments, industries, cultures, and military doctrine.

They are not races.

They are not inherently good or evil.

Each believes its approach represents the safest future.

Each possesses only part of the historical truth.

Factions should disagree through doctrine and incentives rather than
simplistic prejudice.

# Conductors

Conductors do not command machines.

They **conduct will**.

Using a **Relay**, a Conductor lends biological vitality and intent to
nearby automatons.

Machines retain individuality. A Conductor establishes shared purpose
rather than direct puppet-like control.

Conducting has a cost:

-   Fatigue
-   Neurological strain
-   Long-term physical damage
-   Dependence on medical and technical support

Relays are uncommon but not mythical. Governments tightly regulate them
because Conductors are strategic assets and require institutional
support.

# Automatons

Automatons are not mindless drones.

Without a Conductor they continue operating according to routines,
habits, and unfinished directives.

They develop personality through prolonged resonance.

When a Conductor dies, emotional and behavioral echoes remain within the
machines.

Examples include: - protecting children - maintaining abandoned
infrastructure - refusing unnecessary violence - continuing forgotten
patrols

These are not literal memories but resonance scars.

Over time the player's army becomes a cast of characters rather than
disposable units.

# The Player

The player begins as a reclamation technician, not a hero.

A joint expedition investigates and salvages a dormant Caelian relay.

Recovered automatons naturally provide the tutorial roster.

The expedition secretly serves a second purpose: testing recovered
Caelian technology.

During activation the relay synchronizes unexpectedly.

The player survives and becomes a Conductor.

Publicly, the incident is called an accident.

Privately, someone orchestrated the experiment.

The player begins with no faction, no army, and no political backing.

Their first squad consists only of surviving reclaimed machines.

## Motivation

The player is fundamentally a repair engineer.

They solve immediate problems:

-   restore infrastructure
-   rescue civilians
-   repair relays
-   keep systems functioning

They rarely seek political power.

Their philosophy is:

> Fix what is broken.

# Cassian

Cassian begins as the expedition's logistics adjutant.

He survives because the player saves him.

He never becomes a Conductor and does not want a Relay.

His strength is organization.

Initially he protects the player by navigating investigations,
negotiations, and competing faction claims.

As the story progresses, every battlefield victory creates political
consequences.

Cassian turns those victories into lasting agreements.

He gains influence through:

-   negotiation
-   public perception
-   information control
-   coalition building
-   favors
-   diplomacy
-   administrative competence

The player creates leverage.

Cassian spends it.

Eventually Cassian's coalition becomes more powerful than any individual
faction.

# Player vs Cassian

This relationship is the heart of the story.

The player asks:

> Does this solve today's problem?

Cassian asks:

> What precedent does this create tomorrow?

The player repairs civilization.

Cassian reorganizes civilization.

Neither is wrong.

Neither is the villain.

By the finale Cassian no longer needs the player politically.

That is success, not betrayal.

His goal has always been institutions that outlive extraordinary
individuals.

# The Source

The Source should remain mysterious for most of the campaign.

Early understanding: - ancient energy system - strategically
invaluable - politically contested

Late revelation: The Source is not a generator but a balancing and
distribution network integrating multiple energy processes.

The modern factions each preserve only fragments of the original Caelian
system.

# Narrative Structure

The story is intentionally scoped for expansion.

## Initial Release

### Act I -- Reclamation

Tutorial and origin.

-   Salvage expedition
-   Recover automatons
-   Relay activation
-   Player becomes Conductor
-   Escape

### Act II -- The Crisis

One interconnected political crisis involving every faction.

Focus: - ownership of reclaimed technology - political influence - rise
of Cassian - discovery of Caelian inconsistencies

Ends with discovery that Caelis still exists.

### Act III -- Caelis

Late-game campaign.

Major locations: - Outer City - Imperial Archive - Conductor Vault -
Civic Core - The Source

Cassian arrives with a coalition rather than an army.

Final conflict is ideological.

# Canon Ending (Initial Release)

## Accord

The player supports Cassian's settlement.

Partial truth becomes public.

The most destabilizing evidence remains sealed.

The Source becomes governed by a new coalition authority.

The war ends.

Infrastructure survives.

Cassian becomes remembered as the architect of peace.

The player's role is minimized.

History remembers institutions more readily than individuals.

Only after the epilogue do historians refer to the conflict as:

> **The Resonance War**

# Mission Design Guidelines

Every mission should accomplish at least one objective:

-   teach gameplay
-   advance a major character
-   reveal political information
-   reveal historical information
-   change faction relationships
-   strengthen or weaken Cassian's influence
-   create lasting consequences

Avoid filler.

Winning battles should not automatically solve political problems.

# Expansion Philosophy

Do not attempt to tell the entire history in the first release.

Design the world so future expansions naturally expand both gameplay and
narrative.

Expansions should: - introduce new automatons and Conductors - revisit
existing factions from new perspectives - reveal additional Caelian
history - explore unresolved political consequences - introduce new
campaign arcs rather than simply extending mission count

Each expansion should answer one major question while introducing
another.

The goal is a world that feels larger than the player's initial campaign
and continues evolving over time without invalidating earlier events.

# Design Principles

-   Avoid chosen-one narratives.
-   Avoid purely evil antagonists.
-   Avoid simplistic faction stereotypes.
-   Practical success and political success are different.
-   Machines are companions, not disposable tools.
-   Repair is as important as combat.
-   History is incomplete and contested.
-   Institutions matter more than individuals in the long term.
-   Every major revelation should recontextualize earlier events.
