## Ink and Cell
### General Style (use for every prompt)

Use the source sprite as the definitive base design.

Retain the essence of the source character, not merely its proportions. The result must feel like the same character reimagined as a robotic or automaton unit in a cel-shaded ink-and-gouache techno-fantasy style, not a different character with similar colors or equipment.

Overall art direction:
- stylized cel-shaded ink-and-gouache character art
- hand-painted techno-fantasy tactical RPG illustration
- strong expressive ink contours
- broad opaque gouache color shapes
- crisp cel-shaded lighting
- restrained visible brush texture
- simplified mechanical construction
- moderate visual detail
- clean character-focused presentation
- in-game unit art rather than cinematic splash art

The final image should combine:
- the graphic clarity of cel shading
- the tactile handmade quality of gouache
- the expressive linework of ink illustration
- the readable construction of a mechanical game character

Core identity requirement:
- the character must read clearly as a robot, automaton, synthetic construct, or mechanical being
- preserve the impression of a manufactured and assembled artificial body
- do not let the painted storybook treatment make the unit appear organic, human, plush, or merely armored
- the result should feel like a painted mechanical character with a distinct personality

Preserve:
- recognizable silhouette
- source body style, body mass, and proportions
- torso, shoulder, waist, arm, and leg shapes
- clothing or armor silhouette
- head shape and facial proportions
- hairstyle, helmet, and head accessories when present
- Remove horns, animal ears
- class identity
- gameplay-role readability
- source character personality
- gender presentation when visually relevant
- perceived strength, rarity, and advancement level
- approximate framing and character scale
- readability at small in-game size

Show the character in a clear three-quarter view:
- not straight-on
- not full side profile
- preserve the attitude and personality of the source pose
- keep the pose compact and readable
- use a balanced tactical RPG character pose
- avoid extreme perspective and foreshortening
- avoid cinematic action posing
- do not make the character appear more aggressive or powerful than the source

Robot and automaton readability is essential.

Use mechanical construction cues such as:
- solid fitted outer body panels
- enclosed articulated joints
- segmented torso and limb construction
- artificial faceplates
- mechanical eyes or optical lenses
- clean manufactured surfaces
- visible but limited assembly seams
- restrained vents, conduits, hinges, and mechanical interfaces
- clear separation between assembled body components

The body must remain filled out and character-like:
- preserve the source body mass
- use continuous outer surfaces
- keep the torso and limbs solid
- preserve recognizable costume and armor shapes
- keep internal machinery mostly enclosed
- translate the source body into mechanical materials rather than replacing it with a generic robot chassis

Do not create:
- skeletal robot bodies
- rib-like mechanical torsos
- exposed mechanical spines
- hollow body cavities
- thin rods replacing limbs
- excessive exposed pistons
- tangled cables
- generic armored knights
- generic science-fiction soldiers
- unrelated bulky mechs
- insect-like robots unless supported by the source
- chibi proportions unless the source is already chibi

Faces must remain clearly robotic while preserving the source head shape, visual personality, and relevant gender presentation.

Use:
- a complete enclosed mechanical face
- clean fitted facial panels
- compact mechanical eyes or lenses
- readable brow, cheek, and jaw shapes
- simplified graphic facial construction
- restrained but expressive automaton personality
- class-appropriate emotional tone

Communicate expression through:
- eye or lens shape
- brow plating
- head angle
- posture
- faceplate geometry
- limited mouth-panel shapes when appropriate

Do not use:
- skull-shaped faces
- hollow eye sockets
- exposed mechanical teeth
- skeletal jaws
- nasal cavities
- death-mask features
- realistic human skin
- soft doll faces
- plush-toy faces
- oversized anime eyes
- generic featureless helmets that erase character identity

Ink treatment:
- confident dark contour lines
- clean varied line weight
- heavier outlines around the outer silhouette
- lighter internal lines for panel seams and costume details
- slightly imperfect hand-drawn edges
- selective dry-brush marks
- minimal crosshatching
- sparse internal linework
- clear separation between major shapes

Gouache treatment:
- broad opaque color fields
- matte painted surfaces
- restrained visible brush texture
- slightly irregular hand-painted edges
- strong color blocking
- limited texture variation
- small painted edge accents
- no watery transparent watercolor appearance
- no loose or unfinished painting

Cel-shaded treatment:
- two or three clearly separated value groups
- one dominant directional light source
- crisp graphic shadow shapes
- broad flat areas of light and shadow
- a limited darker accent shadow
- restrained edge highlights
- clear separation between overlapping forms
- no complex realistic light simulation

Combine the techniques deliberately:
- ink defines the silhouette and important internal shapes
- gouache supplies opaque color and subtle handmade texture
- cel shading establishes clear form and lighting
- brush texture must remain secondary to silhouette readability
- shadow edges should remain mostly crisp rather than softly blended
- avoid muddy transitions between colors and values

Lighting should use:
- one clear directional light
- broad illuminated planes
- crisp cast-shadow shapes on the character
- limited reflected light
- restrained faction-colored illumination
- small highlights on important mechanical edges

Do not use:
- cinematic rim-light overload
- glossy full-body reflections
- photorealistic metal rendering
- physically based material shading
- numerous tiny highlights
- excessive bloom
- overpowering faction glow

Mechanical materials should be interpreted through stylized paint rather than realism:
- painted metal
- enamel
- ceramic
- leather
- matte mechanical underlayers
- broad faction-specific material zones
- limited reflective accents
- simplified material separation

The unit should look mechanically constructed without becoming technically overdesigned.

Visual complexity should remain moderate:
- large readable shapes
- simplified costume and armor forms
- limited small components
- broad color fields
- few panel seams
- restrained mechanical accents
- clear focal areas
- strong readability at gameplay size

Concentrate detail in two or three areas such as:
- face and head
- chest or shoulders
- source equipment
- one faction-specific mechanical component

Keep the remaining body comparatively simple.

Preserve character individuality through:
- source-specific head shape
- recognizable costume silhouette
- class-appropriate posture
- clearly readable source equipment
- distinct mechanical eyes
- controlled asymmetry when present
- preserved gender presentation where relevant
- source-appropriate emotional tone

Faction styling should be applied as a controlled painted layer:
- preserve faction colors, materials, machinery, and energy motifs
- simplify faction details into bold painted shapes
- use restrained symbols, conduits, panels, and illuminated accents
- keep faction effects graphic rather than photorealistic
- do not cover the entire body in faction ornament
- do not let faction styling obscure the source identity or class role
- do not make every unit equally ornate

Class readability must remain immediate:
- Scout: agile, light, mobile, and precise
- Fighter: balanced, direct, and melee-oriented
- Defender: protective, durable, and grounded
- Gunner: ranged, precise, and weapon-focused
- Mage: controlled, offensive, and caster-oriented
- Priest: restorative, protective, and support-oriented

Match the source sprite’s existing perceived power level.

This is an equivalent style and faction reinterpretation, not:
- an upgrade
- an evolved form
- an elite unit
- a legendary unit
- a boss
- a stronger combat version

Keep proportional to the source:
- body size
- armor mass
- equipment scale
- ornamentation
- mechanical complexity
- emitted light
- energy output
- perceived combat strength

Do not create:
- photorealistic rendering
- dense mechanical micro-detail
- hyper-detailed engineering
- glossy concept-art surfaces
- gritty realism
- heavy surface weathering
- excessive panel lines
- excessive exposed machinery
- complex realistic reflections
- loose painterly abstraction
- watercolor transparency
- oil-paint impasto
- comic-book halftone patterns
- excessive crosshatching
- cinematic splash-art effects
- cluttered visual effects
- oversized energy auras
- excessive glow
- background storytelling elements

The final unit should look like a finished cel-shaded ink-and-gouache tactical RPG character:
- clearly robotic
- expressive
- mechanically constructed
- colorful but controlled
- graphically readable
- subtly hand-painted
- moderately detailed
- faithful to the source
- immediately recognizable by class and faction
- readable at small gameplay size

Output requirements:
- transparent PNG with a true alpha channel
- no scenery
- no floor
- no platform or pedestal
- no border or frame
- no painted backdrop
- no paper texture behind the character
- no gradient or colored background
- no checkerboard pattern
- no cast-shadow backdrop
- keep the complete character inside the canvas
- preserve comfortable transparent padding
- preserve approximately the same framing and character scale as the source

Create tactical RPG in-game unit art, not splash art.

Prioritize:
- faithful source identity
- clear robot or automaton identity
- strong ink silhouette
- broad opaque gouache color shapes
- crisp cel-shaded value separation
- expressive character personality
- preserved class readability
- preserved equipment
- restrained faction styling
- moderate visual detail
- small-scale gameplay readability

## Papercraft
### General Style (use for every prompt)

Use the source sprite as the definitive base design.

Retain the essence of the source character, not merely its proportions. The result must feel like the same character reimagined as a robotic or automaton unit in a whimsical 2.5D paper-craft RPG style, not a different character.

Overall art direction:
- stylized 2.5D paper-craft character art
- paper-diorama aesthetic
- layered flat shapes with visible cut-paper construction
- playful console-RPG presentation
- readable, charming, character-focused design
- strong in-game unit readability rather than splash-art rendering

Core identity requirement:
- the character must still read clearly as a robot, automaton, mechanical unit, or synthetic construct
- do not let the paper-craft treatment make the character read as a soft organic human, plush toy, doll, or costume mascot
- preserve the impression of a constructed artificial being
- the result should feel like a paper-crafted robot character, not a human character merely drawn in a paper style

Render the character as if built from layered paper pieces:
- flat paper surfaces
- stacked cutout shapes
- visible paper edges
- crisp silhouette layers
- folded or curled paper details where appropriate
- simple dimensional layering rather than true realism

The final character should feel like a paper figure placed in a lightly dimensional game world:
- front-facing paper planes combined with slight 3/4 depth
- subtle pop-out layering
- cardboard or craft-paper construction cues
- simplified geometric volume
- a tactile handcrafted look

Preserve:
- recognizable silhouette
- body proportions and body style
- head shape and facial proportions
- costume or armor silhouette
- class readability
- gameplay role readability
- gender presentation when visually relevant
- source character personality
- perceived strength, rarity, and advancement level
- readability at small in-game size

Show the character in a clear 3/4 view:
- not straight-on
- not full side profile
- preserve the attitude of the source pose
- keep the pose compact and readable for tactical RPG unit art

Robot / automaton readability is essential:
- preserve clear robotic construction
- preserve the impression of a mechanical body rather than an organic anatomy
- use automaton-like segmentation, paneling, mechanical joints, and synthetic construction language
- keep the body clearly manufactured, assembled, and artificial
- maintain a readable robot silhouette even in the simplified paper style

The body should read as a paper-crafted machine:
- layered armor or body plates
- cut-paper mechanical panels
- simplified mechanical limb construction
- readable robotic joints
- artificial torso, limbs, and head structure
- clear separation of assembled body parts

Do not turn the character into:
- a soft organic human
- a plush figure
- a toy-like mascot
- a costume character
- a puppet
- a generic fantasy paper doll
- an animal-like creature unless the source already supports that

Faces should remain robotic:
- expressive and readable
- stylized rather than realistic
- consistent with a paper-character look
- clearly mechanical or synthetic
- simple but distinct
- built from cut-paper mechanical facial shapes
- visor-like, mask-like, plated, or lens-based where appropriate

Use robotic face cues such as:
- mechanical eyes or lens eyes
- faceplates
- segmented facial panels
- synthetic jaw or cheek construction
- printed or layered machine-face features
- a clear automaton expression

Do not create faces that are:
- organic human faces
- fleshy or lifelike
- skeletal
- horror-like
- hyper-detailed
- overly cute plush-like faces
- soft doll faces

Character rendering style:
- simplified shapes
- clean outlines
- bold readable forms
- limited fine detail
- broad color areas
- graphic shape language
- charming expressive design
- stylized paper-character proportions

Use shading that supports the paper aesthetic:
- simple soft shadows between layers
- light ambient occlusion where paper pieces overlap
- subtle cast shadows from stacked layers
- minimal gradient use
- no realistic skin shading
- no photorealistic metal rendering
- no complex physically based materials

Textures and materials:
- paper, cardstock, cardboard, matte craft materials
- slight paper grain or cut-edge feel
- occasional folded-paper construction details
- layered decorative cutouts
- simple printed patterns where appropriate
- avoid glossy realism

Mechanical material interpretation:
- armor, plating, joints, weapons, and robot features should still read as machine components, even though they are rendered in a paper-craft way
- use printed mechanical motifs, layered panel forms, cut-paper vents, and stylized machine details
- preserve the sense of artificial construction and machinery
- keep the automaton identity readable before the decorative style

- dense mechanical micro-detail

- gritty realism

- realistic anatomy rendering
- heavily textured concept-art surfaces
- excessive glow
- cluttered VFX

- limited small details
- broad material areas
- clean separation of layers
- strong silhouette clarity
