# Environment Image Generation Prompts

Each environment prompt is assembled from two blocks. Paste Section 1 (always), then the one matching faction block from Section 2. For board backgrounds, optionally append the matching scene-type block from Section 3. Do not attach unit sprites; describe the location instead.

## Section 1: General Style (use for every prompt)

Create a tactical RPG play-environment background in the same cel-shaded ink-and-gouache techno-fantasy style as the game's unit art.

Overall art direction:
- stylized cel-shaded ink-and-gouache environment art
- hand-painted techno-fantasy tactical RPG background
- strong expressive ink contours on architecture and terrain
- broad opaque gouache color shapes
- crisp cel-shaded lighting
- restrained visible brush texture
- simplified mechanical construction
- moderate visual detail
- clean scene-focused presentation
- in-game background art rather than cinematic key art

The final image should combine:
- the graphic clarity of cel shading
- the tactile handmade quality of gouache
- the expressive linework of ink illustration
- the readable construction of a mechanical game environment

Ink treatment:
- confident dark contour lines around buildings, terrain, and large props
- clean varied line weight
- heavier outlines on the main silhouette shapes
- lighter internal lines for panel seams, masonry, and mechanical details
- slightly imperfect hand-drawn edges
- selective dry-brush marks
- minimal crosshatching
- sparse internal linework in the gameplay zone
- clear separation between major shapes

Gouache treatment:
- broad opaque color fields
- matte painted surfaces
- restrained visible brush texture
- slightly irregular hand-painted edges
- strong color blocking
- limited texture variation
- no watery transparent watercolor appearance
- no loose or unfinished painting

Cel-shaded treatment:
- two or three clearly separated value groups
- one dominant directional light source
- crisp graphic shadow shapes
- broad flat areas of light and shadow
- a limited darker accent shadow
- restrained edge highlights
- no complex realistic light simulation

Lighting should use:
- one clear directional light
- broad illuminated planes
- crisp cast-shadow shapes from architecture and terrain
- limited reflected light
- restrained faction-colored illumination
- small highlights on important mechanical edges

Do not use:
- cinematic rim-light overload
- photorealistic material rendering
- physically based shading
- numerous tiny highlights
- excessive bloom
- overpowering faction glow
- photorealistic sky photography
- matte-painting realism

Gameplay readability is the primary constraint.

This background sits behind a grid-based battlefield:
- the center of the image is the gameplay zone where unit sprites and a deployment grid are overlaid
- keep the gameplay zone calm, low-contrast, and low-detail
- keep strong value contrast and fine detail in the periphery and background depth
- keep the horizon and major structural shapes above or below the gameplay zone
- avoid strong focal points, bright glows, or high-detail machinery in the center
- avoid visual elements that mimic unit silhouettes (statues, figures, mannequins)
- keep floor or ground surfaces in the gameplay zone flat and evenly lit
- do not paint a grid, board lines, or cell markings into the image

Composition:
- wide horizontal landscape composition
- clear foreground floor plane, midground structures, background sky or depth layer
- three-quarter elevated camera view, as if looking slightly down at a stage
- no extreme perspective, fisheye, or dramatic camera angles
- stable, level horizon
- the scene should feel like a believable place, not an abstract backdrop

Visual complexity should remain moderate:
- large readable shapes
- simplified architectural and mechanical forms
- broad color fields
- limited small components
- restrained faction accents
- clear focal areas away from the gameplay zone

Concentrate detail in two or three areas such as:
- a signature faction structure in the midground
- a skyline or horizon feature
- one faction-specific mechanical installation

Keep the rest of the scene comparatively simple.

Do not create:
- photorealistic rendering
- dense mechanical micro-detail
- hyper-detailed engineering
- glossy concept-art surfaces
- gritty realism
- heavy surface weathering beyond the faction's identity
- loose painterly abstraction
- watercolor transparency
- oil-paint impasto
- comic-book halftone patterns
- cinematic movie-poster drama
- cluttered visual effects
- oversized energy effects dominating the scene

The scene must never contain:
- characters, units, robots, or people
- crowds or silhouettes of figures
- text, signs with lettering, logos, or banners with writing
- UI elements, frames, or borders
- vignettes that darken the gameplay zone

The final environment should look like a finished cel-shaded ink-and-gouache tactical RPG background:
- graphically readable
- subtly hand-painted
- moderately detailed
- clearly faction-themed
- calm and even in the gameplay zone
- cohesive with the game's unit art style

Output requirements:
- opaque full-bleed background, no transparency
- wide horizontal aspect ratio, at least 16:9
- no border or frame
- no painted frame or decorative edge
- no checkerboard pattern
- no grid lines or board markings
- no characters or units
- keep the complete scene inside the canvas

Create tactical RPG in-game background art, not splash art or key art.

Prioritize:
- strong faction identity
- ink-and-gouache style consistency with the unit art
- gameplay-zone readability
- broad opaque gouache color shapes
- crisp cel-shaded value separation
- moderate visual detail
- believable environment construction

## Section 2: Faction Specification (paste only the matching faction)

### Solar

Environment read: radiant ceremonial sun-powered citadel.
Use clean white-and-gold architecture.
Use polished enamel-like surfaces on buildings and floors.
Use blue-black photovoltaic panel arrays integrated into rooftops, walls, and walkways.
Use bold geometric sun motifs and ray patterns in floors, gates, and facades.
Use amber as the only glow color, shining from seams, lamps, windows, and conduit channels.
Use a bright warm sky with visible light rays and restrained lens-flare accents.
Use floating or elevated platforms, ceremonial bridges, and tiered plazas.
Keep the scene poised, orderly, and ceremonial.
Do not use steam, smoke, or soot.
Do not use industrial grime or decay.
Do not use green circuit traces.

### Steampunk

Environment read: polished brass pressure-driven industrial city, well maintained.
Use polished brass, copper, and bronze as the dominant materials.
Use green verdigris patina on the edges and recesses of copper structures.
Use dark steel only as an accent.
Use cobbled or riveted metal floors, workshops, and courtyard plazas.
Use visible brass gears, clockwork mechanisms, and pipe networks on building facades.
Use smokestacks and exhaust pipes venting clean white steam.
Use multiple gauges with readable dials mounted on walls and machines.
Use prominent brass rivets on beams, plates, and walkways.
Use faint warm light inside gauge glass, windows, and workshop interiors.
Use drifting white steam as the signature atmosphere effect.
Keep the machinery well maintained aside from the verdigris patina.
Do not use soot, ash, or scorch marks.
Do not use burning coal, embers, or open fire.
Do not use blackened or heat-stained metal.
Do not use glowing green circuit traces.

### Coal

Environment read: dirty, worn, soot-covered combustion industry.
Use blackened steel and dark iron structures worn by heat and hard use.
Use visible fire, glowing burning coal, soot, and ash throughout the scene.
Use furnace halls, slag heaps, coke ovens, and firebox structures with burning coal visible behind grates.
Use glowing molten seams and ember cracks in floors, walls, and machinery.
Use furnace-orange and ember red as the only glow colors.
Use heavy smoke pouring from exhaust stacks and vents.
Use falling ash and drifting ember sparks in the air.
Use matte heat-stained surfaces with scorch marks and soot stains.
Use ash dusting on rooftops, ledges, and flat surfaces.
Keep the sky dark and smoke-hazed with an orange underlight.
Keep the gameplay zone readable through the fire and smoke.
Do not use brass, copper, or polished metals.
Do not use clean or well-maintained surfaces.
Do not use bright daylight or clear blue sky.

### Wind

Environment read: light, fast, aerodynamic high-altitude structures.
Use pale blue, silver, and white architecture.
Use lightweight construction with aerodynamic curves and swept-back shapes.
Use compact turbines, rotors, narrow fins, vanes, and vent details on towers and platforms.
Use spinning windmills, rotor arrays, or turbine farms in the midground.
Use visible swirling air currents and wind trails through the scene.
Use flowing cloth-like mechanical streamers and wind banners on poles and masts.
Use small spinning vortex effects near vents and turbines.
Use open sky, high platforms, sky bridges, and floating dock structures.
Use pale blue as the only glow color, in airflow lines tracing the structures.
Use wind-swept shapes that suggest constant motion even in still air.
Do not use sun motifs or solar panels.
Do not use steam, smoke, or soot.
Do not use heavy blocky architecture.

### Fusion

Environment read: dark high-tech reactor facility with neon-green energy.
Use dark matte slate, graphite, and near-black structures.
Use smooth dark high-tech surfaces with a subtle grid texture.
Use thin neon-green circuit-trace seams on floors, walls, and towers.
Use dense motherboard patterns on large flat surfaces.
Use one compact reactor-inspired installation with green containment light as the focal structure.
Use bright glowing energy conduits running beneath surfaces and along walls.
Use green as the only glow color, in plasma indicators, conduits, and holograms.
Use floating holographic screens and data rings as restrained accents.
Use a dark night sky or enclosed facility depth with green ambient light.
Do not use white architecture.
Do not use solar panels or photovoltaic cells.
Do not use brass, copper, rivets, gauges, or steam.
Do not use soot, embers, or fire.
Do not use wind trails or airflow effects.
Do not use a giant reactor core dominating the scene.
Do not use large orbiting rings.
Do not use excessive circuitry.
Do not use photorealistic sci-fi micro-detail.

### Neutral

Environment read: a faction-free shared space, usable by any side.
Use muted desaturated tones: warm grays, soft browns, dull stone, and weathered wood.
Use generic mechanical construction with no faction identity: plain riveted plates, simple beams, unmarked pipes, and workaday machinery.
Use natural materials alongside the machinery: stone, brick, timber, canvas, and rope.
Use neutral warm daylight or soft overcast light with no colored glow.
Use plain lamps, braziers, or window light as the only light accents, in a soft warm white.
Keep the architecture functional and vernacular: workshops, training grounds, market squares, courtyards, and commons.
Use restrained, widely spaced props: crates, benches, tool racks, carts, and awnings.
Keep the scene calm, welcoming, and unaligned with any faction.
Do not use any faction glow color: no amber, furnace-orange, pale blue, or neon-green light.
Do not use photovoltaic panels or sun motifs.
Do not use brass clockwork, gauges, or steam vents as a theme.
Do not use burning coal, molten seams, soot, or ash.
Do not use turbines, rotors, or wind trails.
Do not use circuit-trace seams, reactor cores, or holograms.
Do not use heraldry, banners, or faction symbols.

### All Factions

Environment read: one wide world view where every faction occupies its own region.
Divide the scene into distinct faction regions arranged along the horizon, one region per faction.
Keep the foreground neutral and shared by all factions, as described by the scene-type block.
Give each faction region its signature materials, glow color, architecture, and atmosphere:
- Solar region: clean white-and-gold citadel structures, photovoltaic panel arrays, amber glow, warm radiant light.
- Steampunk region: polished brass and copper machinery, green verdigris patina, gauges, rivets, clean white steam.
- Coal region: blackened steel and dark iron industry, burning coal, molten seams, furnace-orange glow, smoke and falling ash.
- Wind region: pale blue, silver, and white aerodynamic towers, turbines and rotors, pale blue airflow lines, open sky.
- Fusion region: dark matte high-tech structures, thin neon-green circuit-trace seams, one compact reactor installation, green glow.
Keep each region clearly readable as its faction at a glance.
Blend the regions into one another gradually at their borders, as natural transitions of terrain and architecture, not hard vertical stripes.
Keep every faction's visual weight roughly equal so no single faction dominates the scene.
Use one shared sky that shifts tone smoothly across the regions, from warm dawn over Solar to dark night over Fusion.
Use one consistent directional light source across the whole scene.
Keep the faction-specific do-not rules inside each region:
- no steam or soot in the Solar region
- no soot, fire, or embers in the Steampunk region
- no brass or polished metal in the Coal region
- no solar panels or heavy blocky architecture in the Wind region
- no steam, fire, or white architecture in the Fusion region
Do not merge faction motifs across regions (no brass in the Fusion region, no green circuitry in the Solar region).
Do not let the transitions become a rainbow gradient.
Do not let any region's glow or smoke spill heavily into the neutral foreground.

## Section 3: Scene Type (optional, paste only the matching type)

### Main Menu

The scene is the main menu backdrop, pairing the All Factions world view with a neutral foreground.
Keep the bottom third of the image a neutral foreground space shared by no single faction:
- simple, calm ground such as a stone terrace, open plain, or deck edge
- muted desaturated tones with no faction colors, glows, or motifs
- minimal detail, low contrast, and even lighting
- clear open space on one side for the game title and menu UI
- dark and quiet enough for readable UI text and buttons
Place all faction regions in the middle and upper portions of the image, behind the neutral foreground.
Let the foreground lead the eye toward the faction regions on the horizon, like a vantage point overlooking the world.
Use more depth and atmosphere than a battle board.
Do not use characters, units, or figures.
Do not place faction structures, smoke, or glow inside the neutral foreground.
Do not place UI elements, logos, or text in the image.

### Battle Board

The scene is a battlefield background shown behind the deployment grid for the entire match.
Use a grounded floor plane suitable as the base of the play area.
Keep structures and tall elements in the midground and background, behind the play area.
Keep the floor in the gameplay zone uncluttered and evenly lit.
Frame the play area loosely with architecture, walls, or terrain edges.
Do not place obstacles, props, or machinery inside the central gameplay zone.
Do not suggest a specific grid size or lane layout in the painted geometry.

### Menu Backdrop

The scene is a wide atmospheric backdrop for a menu screen, with no grid overlaid.
Use more depth and atmosphere than a battle board.
Allow one strong focal structure in the midground.
Use open space on one side for menu UI placement.
Keep the bottom third calm and dark enough for readable UI text.
Do not use characters, units, or figures.
