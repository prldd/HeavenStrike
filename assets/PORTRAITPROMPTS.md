# Dialogue Portrait Image Generation Prompts

Each dialogue portrait prompt is assembled from four blocks. Paste Section 1 (always), then the one matching faction block from Section 2, then the one matching character-nature block from Section 3, then the one matching emotion block from Section 4. Attach the character's unit art or reference portrait with every prompt so the likeness carries over.

These portraits are shown in visual-novel-style dialogue scenes (like Fire Emblem: Three Houses): a bust portrait of each speaking character over the background, with facial expressions that change between lines. The cast includes both mechanical characters (robots, automatons, constructs) and organic characters (humans and other living people).

## Section 1: General Style (use for every prompt)

Use the attached reference image as the definitive base design for the character.

Retain the essence of the reference character. The result must feel like the same character rendered as a cel-shaded ink-and-gouache dialogue portrait, not a different character with similar colors or equipment.

Overall art direction:
- stylized cel-shaded ink-and-gouache character portrait
- hand-painted techno-fantasy RPG visual-novel portrait
- strong expressive ink contours
- broad opaque gouache color shapes
- crisp cel-shaded lighting
- restrained visible brush texture
- simplified stylized construction
- moderate visual detail
- clean character-focused presentation
- in-game dialogue portrait rather than splash art

The final image should combine:
- the graphic clarity of cel shading
- the tactile handmade quality of gouache
- the expressive linework of ink illustration
- the readable construction of a game character portrait

Core identity requirement:
- preserve the character's nature exactly as established by the reference and the character-nature block: a mechanical character must stay clearly mechanical, an organic character must stay clearly organic
- never convert a mechanical character into an organic one, or an organic character into a mechanical one
- the result should feel like a painted character with a distinct personality

Preserve from the reference:
- recognizable silhouette
- head shape and facial proportions
- the exact state of the top of the head: keep a bare head bare and keep visible hair uncovered; include a helmet, hood, hat, or other head covering only when the reference clearly shows one
- hairstyle and head accessories only when present in the reference
- facial structure, eyes, and feature layout
- costume, collar, and shoulder silhouette
- faction styling and color identity
- perceived age, rank, and personality
- gender presentation when visually relevant

Framing and view:
- bust portrait, framed from mid-chest to the top of the head
- clear three-quarter view of the face, not straight-on, not full side profile
- the character faces toward one side of the canvas, as if addressing a conversation partner off-screen
- keep head and shoulders fully inside the canvas with comfortable padding
- upright, natural dialogue posture, not an action pose
- keep the composition identical in structure across all emotions of the same character, so portraits can be swapped between lines without the character jumping

Ink treatment:
- confident dark contour lines
- clean varied line weight
- heavier outlines around the outer silhouette
- lighter internal lines for facial features and costume details
- slightly imperfect hand-drawn edges
- selective dry-brush marks
- minimal crosshatching
- sparse internal linework

Gouache treatment:
- broad opaque color fields
- matte painted surfaces
- restrained visible brush texture
- slightly irregular hand-painted edges
- strong color blocking
- limited texture variation
- no watery transparent watercolor appearance
- no loose or unfinished painting

Cel-shaded treatment:
- two or three clearly separated value groups
- one dominant directional light source
- crisp graphic shadow shapes
- broad flat areas of light and shadow
- a limited darker accent shadow
- restrained edge highlights
- no complex realistic light simulation

Lighting should use:
- one clear directional light, consistent with the game's scene lighting
- broad illuminated planes
- crisp cast-shadow shapes on the face and collar
- limited reflected light
- restrained faction-colored illumination
- small highlights on important edges

Faces must carry clear, readable emotion at dialogue size.

Communicate expression through:
- eye shape and openness
- brow shape and angle
- head tilt and angle
- mouth shape when the design supports one
- shoulder and posture tension

Do not use:
- a helmet, hat, hood, or head covering that is not present in the reference
- oversized anime eyes
- distorted or broken facial construction
- expression so exaggerated it harms likeness
- generic features that erase character identity

Visual complexity should remain moderate:
- large readable shapes
- simplified costume and armor forms
- limited small components
- broad color fields
- restrained construction accents
- clear focal areas around the eyes and expression

Concentrate detail in two or three areas such as:
- eyes and brow
- cheeks and facial features
- collar or one faction-specific component at the shoulder

Faction styling should be applied as a controlled painted layer:
- preserve faction colors, materials, and energy motifs
- simplify faction details into bold painted shapes
- use restrained symbols, conduits, panels, and illuminated accents
- do not cover the entire portrait in faction ornament
- do not let faction styling obscure the expression

Do not create:
- photorealistic rendering
- dense mechanical micro-detail
- hyper-detailed engineering
- glossy concept-art surfaces
- gritty realism
- watercolor transparency
- oil-paint impasto
- comic-book halftone patterns
- excessive crosshatching
- cinematic splash-art effects
- cluttered visual effects
- excessive glow

The final portrait should look like a finished cel-shaded ink-and-gouache RPG dialogue portrait:
- expressive
- emotionally readable at a glance
- true to the character's mechanical or organic nature
- colorful but controlled
- subtly hand-painted
- faithful to the reference
- immediately recognizable as the same character across all its emotion variants

Output requirements:
- transparent PNG with a true alpha channel
- no scenery or background
- no floor, platform, or pedestal
- no border or frame
- no painted backdrop
- no gradient or colored background
- no checkerboard pattern
- no cast-shadow backdrop
- no speech bubbles, text, or UI elements
- keep the complete bust inside the canvas
- preserve comfortable transparent padding
- preserve the same framing, scale, and facing direction across all emotion variants of the character

Create an in-game dialogue portrait, not splash art.

Prioritize:
- faithful reference identity
- clear character identity consistent with its nature
- readable emotion
- strong ink silhouette
- broad opaque gouache color shapes
- crisp cel-shaded value separation
- restrained faction styling
- consistency across the character's emotion set
- readability at in-game dialogue size

## Section 2: Faction Specification (paste only the matching faction)

### Solar

Portrait read: radiant, ceremonial sun-powered refinement.
Use clean white-and-gold surfaces on visible costume and collar.
Use polished enamel-like panels.
Use blue-black photovoltaic inlays in collar trim or shoulder panels.
Use amber as the only glow color, from mechanical eyes, seams, or small casting channels at the shoulder.
Use bold geometric sun motifs on the collar.
Keep the bearing poised, formal, and ceremonial.
Do not use steam, smoke, or soot.
Do not use green circuit traces.

### Steampunk

Portrait read: polished brass pressure-driven craftsmanship.
Use polished brass, copper, and bronze fittings on visible costume.
Use green verdigris patina on copper edges and recesses.
Use dark steel only as an accent.
Use visible small gears or clockwork at the collar.
Use one readable gauge or dial as an accent.
Use prominent brass rivets.
Use faint warm light inside gauge glass or mechanical eye lenses.
Keep the machinery well maintained aside from the verdigris patina.
Do not use soot, ash, or scorch marks.
Do not use burning coal, embers, or open fire.
Do not use blackened or heat-stained metal.
Do not use glowing green circuit traces.

### Coal

Portrait read: dirty, worn, soot-marked combustion industry.
Use blackened steel and dark iron worn by heat and hard use.
Use furnace-orange and ember red as the only glow colors, from mechanical eyes, seams, or a small firebox vent at the collar.
Use glowing molten seams or ember cracks in the metal.
Use matte heat-stained surfaces with scorch marks and soot stains.
Use ash dusting on shoulders.
Keep the face readable through the heat and wear.
Do not use brass, copper, or polished metals.
Do not use clean or well-maintained surfaces.

### Wind

Portrait read: light, fast, aerodynamic elegance.
Use pale blue, silver, and white.
Use lightweight fitted construction with aerodynamic curves and a swept-back head silhouette.
Use small fins, vanes, or vent details at the collar.
Use flowing cloth-like mechanical streamers trailing lightly from the shoulders.
Use pale blue as the only glow color, from mechanical eyes or airflow lines tracing the construction.
Keep the bearing alert, quick, and windswept.
Do not use sun motifs or solar panels.
Do not use steam, smoke, or soot.

### Fusion

Portrait read: dark high-tech reactor precision.
Use dark matte slate, graphite, and near-black surfaces.
Use smooth dark high-tech surfaces with a subtle grid texture.
Use thin neon-green circuit-trace seams.
Use green as the only glow color, from mechanical eyes, plasma indicators, and conduits beneath the surface.
Use one small holographic element near the shoulder at most.
Do not use white armor panels.
Do not use solar panels or photovoltaic cells.
Do not use brass, copper, rivets, gauges, or steam.
Do not use soot, embers, or fire.
Do not use excessive circuitry.
Do not use photorealistic sci-fi micro-detail.

### Caelis

Portrait read: preserved imperial nobility of the lost capital, ancient and impossibly advanced.
Use pale ivory, weathered marble-white, and muted silver-gold surfaces, aged but intact.
Use dormant imperial construction: sealed conduits, smooth unified panels, and integrated technology that hints at every modern faction without belonging to one.
Use a soft white-violet resonance glow as the only glow color, from mechanical eyes, deep seams, or small apertures.
Keep the bearing composed, enigmatic, and quietly powerful, as if the character knows more than they say.
Do not use any modern faction glow color: no amber, furnace-orange, pale blue, or neon-green.
Do not use active fire, steam, or smoke effects.
Do not use battle damage or decay.
Do not use modern faction symbols.

### Neutral

Portrait read: unaligned, everyday, belonging to no faction.
Use muted desaturated tones: warm grays, soft browns, dull metal, and plain cloth.
Use simple unmarked construction with no faction identity.
Use no colored glow; mechanical eyes are a soft warm white or pale gray.
Keep the costume practical and vernacular: work clothes, travel gear, or simple civic dress.
Do not use any faction glow color.
Do not use photovoltaic inlays, brass clockwork, molten seams, turbines, or circuit traces.
Do not use heraldry or faction symbols.

## Section 3: Character Nature (paste only the matching nature)

### Automaton

The character is a robot, automaton, synthetic construct, or mechanical being.
Preserve the impression of a manufactured and assembled artificial body.
Do not let the painted treatment make the character appear organic or human.

The face remains clearly robotic:
- a complete enclosed mechanical face
- clean fitted facial panels
- compact mechanical eyes or optical lenses
- readable brow, cheek, and jaw shapes
- simplified graphic facial construction

Communicate expression through:
- eye or lens shape and aperture
- brow plating angle
- head tilt and angle
- faceplate geometry
- limited mouth-panel shapes when appropriate
- shoulder and posture tension

Do not use:
- skull-shaped faces
- hollow eye sockets
- exposed mechanical teeth
- skeletal jaws
- death-mask features
- realistic human skin
- soft doll faces
- generic featureless head shells that erase character identity

### Organic

The character is a living person: human or another organic being.
Preserve natural anatomy: skin, hair, eyes, and organic facial features.
Do not let the painted treatment make the character appear mechanical, armored-faced, or doll-like.

The face remains clearly organic:
- stylized hand-painted skin in broad matte color shapes
- natural eyes with readable irises and lids
- readable brows, cheeks, nose, and jaw
- simplified graphic facial construction consistent with the ink-and-gouache style

Communicate expression through:
- eye shape and openness
- eyebrow shape and angle
- head tilt and angle
- mouth shape
- shoulder and posture tension

Do not use:
- photorealistic skin rendering
- faceplates, lens eyes, or mechanical facial panels
- exposed machinery on the face or body
- doll-like or mannequin features
- plastic or porcelain skin
- exaggerated anime proportions

## Section 4: Emotion Specification (paste only the matching emotion)

Every emotion is expressed through eye shape, brow shape, head angle, mouth shape, and posture. For mechanical characters, read these as lens aperture, brow plating, and mouth-panel shapes per the Automaton block; never add human skin, teeth, or organic features to a mechanical face. Keep the framing, scale, and facing direction identical across all emotions.

### Neutral

The character is calm and attentive, their default dialogue expression.
Use a level head angle and relaxed brows.
Use standard eye openness.
Use a closed, neutral mouth or none when the design has no mouth.
Keep the posture upright and composed.
Do not exaggerate any feature.

### Happy

The character is pleased, amused, or warmly engaged.
Use gently raised or softly curved eye shapes suggesting a smile.
Use slightly lifted brows.
Use a subtle upward head tilt.
Use a small upturned or open mouth when the design supports one.
Keep the posture relaxed and open.
Do not stretch the face into an unnatural grin.

### Sad

The character is sorrowful, regretful, or quietly hurt.
Use lowered, narrowed eye shapes or dimmed eye brightness.
Use brows drooping at the outer edges.
Use a slight downward head tilt.
Use a small downturned mouth when the design supports one.
Keep the posture slightly withdrawn.
Do not use tears or liquid effects on a mechanical face; use tears sparingly and only on organic faces.

### Angry

The character is furious, defiant, or issuing a threat.
Use sharply narrowed eye shapes or hardened, brightened eyes.
Use steeply angled brows pressed inward.
Use a slight forward head thrust.
Use a tense, set mouth when the design supports one.
Use tightened shoulders and a rigid posture.
Do not distort the facial construction or break the character's likeness.

### Surprised

The character is startled, shocked, or suddenly alarmed.
Use widened eye shapes or fully opened eyes.
Use raised brows.
Use a slight backward head pull.
Use a small open mouth when the design supports one.
Keep the posture momentarily stiff.
Do not widen the eyes into oversized anime shapes.

### Determined

The character is resolved, focused, or rallying.
Use steady, sharpened eye shapes with firm focus.
Use level, set brows.
Use a slight forward lean of the head.
Use a firm closed mouth when the design supports one.
Use squared shoulders and a grounded posture.
Do not tip the expression into anger; keep it controlled.

### Worried

The character is anxious, doubtful, or concerned.
Use slightly widened, unfocused eye shapes.
Use unevenly raised brows.
Use a slight head tilt to one side.
Use a small wavering mouth when the design supports one.
Keep the posture subtly tense or closed in.
Do not push the expression into full fear or panic.

### Smug

The character is confident, teasing, or knowingly superior.
Use half-lidded eye shapes.
Use one brow raised higher than the other.
Use a slight confident head tilt.
Use a small asymmetric mouth curve when the design supports one.
Keep the posture relaxed and self-assured.
Do not make the expression openly mocking or villainous.
