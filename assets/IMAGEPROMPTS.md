# Image Generation Prompts

Each unit prompt is assembled from three blocks. Paste Section 1 (always), then the one matching faction block from Section 2, then the one matching class block from Section 3. Attach the source sprite with every prompt.

## Section 1: General Style (use for every prompt)

Use the source sprite as the definitive base design.
Preserve the recognizable silhouette, body mass, and proportions of the source.
Preserve the torso, shoulder, waist, arm, and leg shapes of the source.
Preserve the head shape, facial proportions, and gender presentation of the source.
Preserve the framing, scale, and canvas placement of the source.
Keep the character readable at small in-game size.
Pose the character in a clear three-quarter view with a compact pose.
Translate the character into robotic materials with a solid body, continuous outer surfaces, and enclosed joints.
Do not change the body type.
The face must keep the source head shape, facial proportions, and visual personality.
Use smooth fitted facial panels and compact mechanical eyes or lenses.
Do not create a chibi or childlike face.
Do not create oversized anime eyes.
Use a medium-detail stylized tactical RPG art style with large clean readable shapes, simplified panels, and broad material areas.
Use limited seams and only a few visible mechanical details.
Use smooth clean surfaces with clear highlights.
Follow the faction specification for glow, effects, and surface treatment; it overrides the general style.
Do not use dense micro-detail.
Do not use excessive panel lines.
Do not use numerous tiny rivets.
Do not use photorealistic materials.
Do not use cinematic concept-art rendering.
Do not cover every surface in highly reflective metal.
Match the existing power level of the source sprite. This is an equivalent faction reinterpretation.
Keep body size, armor mass, equipment scale, ornamentation, mechanical complexity, emitted light, and energy output proportional to the source.
When in doubt, keep the body lightly built.

Keep the entire character inside the canvas with comfortable transparent padding around the silhouette.
Keep approximately the same character scale and framing as the source.
Create tactical RPG in-game unit art, not splash art.
